# Governance operating models for vibe coding and AI-assisted citizen development (as of 9 October 2026)

Scope note for the writer: this research was commissioned to repair the "Organisations converge on amnesty, a paved road and risk tiers" section of the original report in light of the independent audit. The audit's specific asks were: (a) label amnesty/paved-road as a *proposed* operating model, not a proven market approach; (b) show ownership, exceptions, kill-switch design and assurance evidence for the tier model; (c) stop conflating employee-use surveys that measure different things on different populations; (d) replace "no company publishes a practice" with a bounded statement; (e) add owner transfer, offboarding, versioning, exception management and supplier review to the lifecycle. Every section below is organised around those asks. Sources I could not reach, or figures whose primary source I could not locate, are listed under Gaps rather than reported.

Sources reviewed in this pass (for the bounded statements below): 3 Gartner abstracts + 1 Gartner reprint landing page + 2 Gartner press releases; 2 CSA research notes; Forrester 2026 predictions (via SD Times); Microsoft Learn (CoE Starter Kit governance components, tenant hygiene, admin-center security recommendations, adoption framework/maturity model); Cloudflare reference architecture; Method, Tenable, EXANTE first-party pieces; AWS AI-DLC blog; ServiceNow community lifecycle article; Mendix Control Center / Private Mendix Platform docs; Lovable workspace docs; GOV.UK (4 pages), DTA (2 pages), GSA compliance plan, IMY/Digg news page; Netskope 2026 report (+2025 methodology statements), LayerX 2025 report, Harmonic Q2 2025 release, IBM CODB 2025 release, Retool (3 reports), Gartner survey releases, Cyberhaven 2026 report + six-month update, Software AG study (via eWeek), KPMG/Melbourne 2025, Konstanz 2026 (via Pebblous summary), PagerDuty 2026, Zenity 2024, Nokod 2026 (×2); trade press on 2023 bans; plus vendor blogs (Northflank, Qovery) read only for what they disclose about named customers.

## Key question 1: What do Gartner and the CSA actually recommend, in their own words, and what is forecast versus observed?

### Takeaway
Gartner has published at least three vibe-coding governance notes between June and September 2026, all paywalled; the publicly visible abstracts frame vibe coding as a present risk to be "enabled and governed", while the widely quoted 60%/30% figures are Strategic Planning Assumptions (forecasts) that appear only on a vendor reprint page, not in the public abstract. The CSA's June 2026 note recommends lightweight, non-punitive registration and a three-category review model but explicitly does not address amnesty programmes, kill switches or lifecycle, so the original report's attribution of the full amnesty/paved-road/tier package to "analysts and the CSA" overstated what they wrote.

### Cited Findings

**Gartner, "Govern Vibe Coding for Citizen Developers With Self-Service Platforms" (ID 8068765)**
- Published 29 June 2026; analyst Nitish Tyagi. Public abstract (observational framing): business users and citizen developers are increasingly demanding vibe coding tools and AI coding agents (examples named: Lovable, Replit, Claude Code, Codex); "This shift raises security, compliance, and governance risks"; "This note explains how software engineering leaders can enable and govern vibe coding effectively." No predictions, no survey N and no recommendations are visible on the public page; the sections labelled Strategic Planning Assumptions and Impact are paywalled — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/8068765)
- The Tray.ai reprint landing page (page dated 2 September 2026; note date 29 June 2026 per disclaimer) attributes to Gartner: a two-layer model of "development controls" and "pre-deployment validation"; "an adaptive governance framework" for deciding which use cases citizen developers own and which stay with engineering; and agent safeguards — "sandboxing agents, mitigating prompt injection, restricting outbound data flows, and capping token spend". It frames the goal as governing vibe coding "without blocking it" — [Tray.ai](https://tray.ai/guides/gartner-govern-vibe-coding.md)
- The two predictions are on the Tray.ai page only, phrased as forecasts: "by 2028, 60% of vibe-coded applications will be retired" because they "fail to deliver the predictable business value", and "30% of software engineering teams will face material capacity strain from maintaining vibe-coded applications" built by citizen developers and business users. No sample, method or baseline is given — [Tray.ai](https://tray.ai/guides/gartner-govern-vibe-coding.md); not present on [Gartner's public abstract](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/8068765)

**Other Gartner vibe-coding notes (abstracts only)**
- "How to Govern Vibe Coding" (ID 8207229), 31 July 2026, Mike Warren, Hao Yin, Sohail Majumdar. Abstract: "Ungoverned vibe coding creates unmanaged risk, bypassing existing security and governance controls." "Application leaders should use this research to implement continuous governance, securing AI-generated applications while ensuring compliance and financial accountability." — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/8207229)
- "Deliver the Benefits of Vibe Coding Without the Risk With These 4 Low-Code Platform Markets" (ID 8437013), 28 September 2026, Sohail Majumdar and Kelli Smith. Abstract: business users are using vibe coding to customise applications, accelerating development while introducing security and operational risks; low-code/no-code platforms "offer four markets application leaders can choose from" to provide governed citizen development — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/8437013)
- Gartner also publishes a "Market Guide for Citizen Application Development Platforms" (2025), known here only through a vendor press release — [Kintone press release, 30 June 2025](https://www.kintone.com/en-us/press-center/kintone-has-been-recognized-in-gartner-market-guide-for-citizen-application-development-platforms-1/)

**Gartner shadow-AI survey and forecast (primary press releases)**
- Survey of 302 cybersecurity leaders, fieldwork March–May 2025, North America, EMEA and Asia/Pacific: 69% of organisations "suspect or have evidence that employees are using prohibited public GenAI". Forecast (not observation): "by 2030 more than 40% of enterprises will experience security or compliance incidents linked to unauthorized shadow AI." Recommendation (Arun Chandrasekaran): "CIOs should define clear enterprise-wide policies for AI tool usage, conduct regular audits for shadow AI activity and incorporate Gen AI risk evaluation into their SaaS assessment processes" — [Gartner newsroom, 19 Nov 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-11-19-gartner-identifies-critical-genai-blind-spots-that-cios-must-urgently-address0); survey details in [Gartner newsroom, 22 Sep 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-09-22-gartner-survey-reveals-generative-artificial-intelligence-attacks-are-on-the-rise)

**CSA, "The Vibe Coding Governance Gap" (CSA Labs research note, 2 June 2026, CSA AI Safety Initiative)**
- Three review categories (the note does not name them as tiers or attach control lists): (1) "Internal tools handling non-sensitive data"; (2) "Applications processing personally identifiable or regulated data"; (3) "Applications that incorporate AI API calls or agentic functionality". "These three tiers warrant different levels of security review"; the note argues against "a single heavyweight gate" and points to Superblocks and Zenity low-code CoE models as reference architectures for tiered intake without detailing them — [CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-vibe-coding-ai-governance-gap-20260602-csa/)
- Registration: "a lightweight registration process—deliberately not a heavyweight review process at this stage", paired with monitoring for unregistered apps; compliance is more likely "when it is paired with non-punitive framing"; "The goal of this initial step is awareness, not gatekeeping." — [CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-vibe-coding-ai-governance-gap-20260602-csa/)
- Other recommendations: audit AI features in sanctioned platforms (Power Platform, Salesforce, ServiceNow, Google Workspace) for apps handling sensitive data, using governance dashboards the note says are "frequently underutilized"; DLP at the API gateway to detect outbound connections from citizen-built apps to external AI services; mandatory code and secrets scanning before deploying regulated-data apps where source export is supported, otherwise vendor security attestations and audit logging in contracts; training that is "concrete, scenario-based, and tied to the specific platforms employees are using"; vendors should provide mandatory policy templates, deploy-time secrets scanning and required authentication configuration — [CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-vibe-coding-ai-governance-gap-20260602-csa/)
- Not addressed by the note: amnesty programmes, kill switches, and application lifecycle management from creation through retirement — [CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-vibe-coding-ai-governance-gap-20260602-csa/)
- Framework-gap claims: "none of the major AI security frameworks in active use" provide "dedicated, accessible guidance for citizen developers"; NIST AI RMF/AI 600-1: "The framework's actors are organizational entities with documented roles" and a business analyst building on Copilot Studio "has no role defined in the NIST model"; OWASP LLM Top 10 (2025) addresses developers, AppSec teams, CISOs and data scientists only; OWASP Citizen Development Top 10 and Low-Code/No-Code Top 10 exist but "predate vibe coding"; in CSA's own AI Controls Matrix the citizen developer "falls ambiguously between 'Application Provider' and 'AI Customer'"; recommends adding a "Citizen Developer / Non-Technical Deployer" role to AICM — [CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-vibe-coding-ai-governance-gap-20260602-csa/)
- Statistics the note relies on, with what methodology it gives: "56% of employees use unauthorized AI tools at work and only 23% use IT-governed alternatives" — IDC 2025, cited via Vectra AI, no N/population/period; 63% of 600 organisations had no AI governance policy — IBM 2025; 20% shadow-AI-linked breaches / 49% expect one within 12 months — "a separate analysis" via Vectra AI, no method; USD 670K extra cost — IBM 2025 — [CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-vibe-coding-ai-governance-gap-20260602-csa/)

**CSA, "Shadow AI Apps: The Enterprise Attack Surface That Outpaces Monitoring" (30 May 2026, v1.0)**
- Attributed to the CSA AI Safety Initiative; the document states it was "generated with AI assistance" and has not gone through official CSA review; no authors named — [CSA PDF](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/CSA_research_note_shadow_ai_apps_enterprise_20260530-csa-styled.pdf)
- Position on bans: "The goal of governance is redirection of productive behavior into monitored channels, not prohibition of it." It calls provisioning approved alternatives "equally important" to inventory and policy, describes "supply-side governance" as "the highest-leverage response", and treats blocking as one layer (noting enterprises blocked about 60% of Zscaler-observed AI traffic on risk grounds). It repeats a vendor claim (Unseen Security) that sanctioned tools cut unauthorised use by 89%, without N — [CSA PDF](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/CSA_research_note_shadow_ai_apps_enterprise_20260530-csa-styled.pdf)
- Recommendations: AI asset discovery sweep (network telemetry, browser extension scanning, SaaS API audits, including personal-account use); "Assign an AI asset owner"; "Treat inventory gaps as the primary governance metric"; publish an AI Acceptable Use Policy and confirm it reaches all employees; map data tiers (public/internal/confidential/restricted) to permitted tools with restricted data (PHI, PCI, trade secrets) requiring explicit written approval; CASB/SSE, browser-native controls, inline DLP; govern agents (MCP, agent-to-agent, credential persistence) as a separate discipline. Not addressed: amnesty, a formal registry, approval SLAs, kill switches, lifecycle stages — [CSA PDF](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/CSA_research_note_shadow_ai_apps_enterprise_20260530-csa-styled.pdf)
- Caveats the note itself makes: its statistics "come primarily from vendor telemetry and vendor-sponsored research"; a policy's existence without active communication "may have negligible effect"; approved access and governed use are "two distinct states" — [CSA PDF](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/CSA_research_note_shadow_ai_apps_enterprise_20260530-csa-styled.pdf)

**Forrester (only analyst house other than Gartner with a located, relevant publication)**
- 2026 predictions (reported 28 Oct 2025): vibe coding "will evolve into vibe engineering, moving from just generating code to encompassing the full software development lifecycle"; "observability as code and governance as code will be adopted by 80% of development teams". No citizen-developer or app-builder prediction in the article — [SD Times](https://sdtimes.com/softwaredev/forrester-shares-its-predictions-for-how-ai-will-continue-to-shape-software-development-in-2026/)

### Inferences
- Everything quantitative that is routinely attributed to "Gartner on vibe coding" (60% retired, 30% strain) is a forecast reachable only through a vendor reprint; the report should cite it as "Gartner Strategic Planning Assumption, as reproduced by Tray.ai" and mark the public abstract as containing no numbers.
- The CSA's three categories are a review-depth heuristic, not a control framework: no tier has specified controls, owners, exceptions or kill-switch logic. The original report's T0–T3 table with controls is therefore author synthesis built on a CSA skeleton, exactly as the audit said.
- Neither CSA note nor any Gartner abstract uses the word "amnesty"; "non-punitive" is CSA's word for the registration step only. The amnesty/inventory-campaign concept must be labelled as a proposed operating model.

### Gaps
- Full text of all three Gartner notes is paywalled; recommendations beyond the abstracts are known only via Tray.ai's summary.
- No IDC primary document for the 56%/23% statistic was located; it reaches the CSA via Vectra AI. Population, N, period and question wording are unknown.
- No Forrester, IDC, McKinsey, BCG or Deloitte publication specifically on vibe-coding *governance* was found in this pass (Forrester's only relevant item is the prediction above). Treat the analyst base as Gartner plus CSA.

## Key question 2: Which named organisations have published a policy or practice, and what does it contain? (bounded statement)

### Takeaway
Of the roughly 40 sources reviewed, three organisations describe their own vibe-coding/citizen-coding governance in the first person — Method (fintech), Tenable (security vendor) and EXANTE (regulated brokerage) — plus one unnamed "large European insurance company" via AWS. None of the three publishes a registry schema, approval SLA, expiry rule or any outcome metric. Government guidance exists for AI *tools* (UK, Australia, US GSA, Sweden) but none of it addresses citizen-built apps or vibe coding specifically.

### Cited Findings

**Named enterprise disclosures (first-party)**
- **Method** (fintech), "Vibe" internal platform, published 21 April 2026 by Chris Vu, Security Lead. Users (marketing, sales, executive assistants) describe an app at chat.methodfi.dev; the platform builds and deploys inside Method's infrastructure. Apps get "the same access controls and data handling restrictions as any other internal service", no broad access to sensitive customer data and no exceptions to secrets rules; config files declare secret names only, values live in a secret manager and sync to the cluster; secret scanning on every commit; non-root, read-only root filesystem, all Linux capabilities dropped; egress limited to DNS and external HTTPS; deployment blocked on critical dependency/image vulnerabilities; three review layers (automated scanning, automated AI code review on every PR, manual human approval); PR-scoped previews on a temporary subdomain behind the private network, deleted when the PR closes. Reported outcomes are qualitative only ("dozens of demos", one co-branded demo built in 48 hours). Not covered: app ownership, registry/inventory, expiry/decommissioning, total app or user counts, quantified time saved — [Method](https://methodfi.com/resources/method-labs/vibe)
- **Tenable**, 6 October 2026, Phillip Hayes. States "Governance works better than blanket bans, because prohibiting AI-aided development usually causes employees to keep their app development activities hidden." Describes a five-tier AI governance model: Tier 1 Strategy (Executive Staff); Tier 2 Governance (AI Governance Board and AI Technical Council "create AI policies and guidance documentation, such as lists of approved AI tools" and "explicitly own compliance, data privacy, and model risk"); Tier 3 Execution (AI Functional Leads and R&D Champions per department, with "direct authority and accountability over AI skill approvals, operational queues, and organization-wide AI adoption", overseeing "all requests for new skills, connectors, and data access" and tracking ROI); Tier 4 Enablement (IT, L&D, Corporate Communications working group); Tier 5 Community (Slack channels). "Tenable has tied AI tool access directly to mandatory security training and responsible usage training", complemented by live webinars, pre-built courses, tool-specific deep dives and weekly show-and-tell. Also notes that even compliant citizen coders' volume "can become overwhelming and disruptive for the IT and security teams", that token costs "can add up quickly", and that citizen coders "create their own local data lakes", producing "dangerous data sprawl". No registry schema, SLA, expiry, kill switch or metrics are published — [Tenable](https://www.tenable.com/blog/how-to-secure-mitigate-ai-risk-vibe-coding-ai-apps-citizen-coders)
- **EXANTE** (regulated brokerage; "over 700 staff", technology team of 230), 27 February 2026, Richard Forss, CTO, first-person thought-leadership piece. Deliberately encourages all staff (operations, compliance, middle office) to build tools. Guardrails: (1) "proper, governed internal APIs… Not direct database access. Not CSV exports emailed to a shared mailbox"; (2) "managed environments where these small utilities can live, breathe and be looked after", so tools do not end up as "a rogue process on someone's laptop"; (3) "We insist that all vibe-coded work goes into version control" (Git) because inexperienced builders hit dead ends "around iteration 15"; (4) a sharing/discovery mechanism so colleagues can adapt existing tools. No metrics, registry, approval process or expiry described; "We're working on it." — [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/thought-leadership/vibe-coding-in-a-regulated-business-or-how-i-learned-to-stop-worrying-and-let-the-compliance-team-have-kittens/)
- **Unnamed "large European insurance company"** (AWS AI-DLC blog, 23 Sep 2026): applied new UI guidelines via agentic development using Kiro; "the customer's engineers, working with AWS, built the governance layer of the harness and built the skills necessary to maintain it in three days"; "Every rule has a single owner (a human) and traces to an institutional standard"; "Compliance on save: every File-Save triggers policy checks". This concerns professional engineers, not citizen builders — adjacent evidence only — [AWS for Industries](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/industries/how-financial-institutions-can-operationalize-ai-dlc-quickly-and-effectively/)

**Reference architectures (vendor, not disclosed customer practice)**
- **Cloudflare** enterprise vibe-coding platform reference architecture (last updated 28 Sep 2026): metadata store acts as application registry surfacing "the owner, team, description, connected data sources, and usage metrics for each deployment"; cost attribution via resource tagging linking each user worker to its owner (no separate cost-centre field — the original report's "cost centre" wording overstated this); Cloudflare Access (SAML/OIDC) on the platform entrypoint and, in production, "verifies and secures employee or third-party access for the entire custom domain"; "Each User Worker can only access the bindings that are explicitly attached to it"; per-tenant D1/KV/R2; secrets in Secrets Store injected by the outbound handler/worker so "The sandboxed environment never has direct access to credentials"; egress allow/deny; per-invocation limits "by user tier, team, or application classification"; deployment pipeline as the approval workflow with security checks, dependency scans and human-in-the-loop review "where required". Expiry, decommissioning and owner transfer are not covered — [Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/reference-architecture/diagrams/ai/enterprise-ai-vibe-coding-platform/)
- Vendor marketing that names customers but discloses no policy: Northflank names WingBank (Cambodia) as using isolated environments for AI-built apps pending review — [Northflank](https://northflank.com/blog/how-finance-teams-can-safely-deploy-ai-built-applications); Qovery markets publish approvals, a governance proxy and an "emergency kill switch" that "halts all agent traffic org-wide or per-blueprint" — [Qovery](https://www.qovery.com/blog/the-lovable-experience-enterprise-governance-your-infrastructure-we-built-it). Treat both as capability claims, not practice.
- A widely indexed "Case Study 1: Crafting an Enterprise AI Coding Policy" about "NovaTech" is a textbook chapter with an apparently fictional company; it should not be cited as a disclosed practice — [datafield.dev](https://datafield.dev/vibe-coding/part-v-professional/ch35-ip-licensing-legal/case-study-01.html)

**Government guidance (none vibe-coding-specific)**
- **UK**: "AI Insights: AI Coding Assistants for developers in HMG" (GOV.UK, 5 June 2025) names three risks including "the unregulated use of AICA leading to unsanctioned shadow IT"; "The programmer is responsible for code produced"; "Be wary of dependencies introduced by AI coding assistants"; experimentation "strongly encouraged". Audience is developers, not citizen builders — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-insights/ai-insights-ai-coding-assistants-for-developers-in-hmg-html). The Generative AI Framework for HMG (Jan 2024) says of code assistants "You must be certain to only use official plugins" and advises manual licence checks for complex generated algorithms — [GOV.UK PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/65c3b5d628a4a00012d2ba5c/6.8558_CO_Generative_AI_Framework_Report_v7_WEB.pdf). The AI Playbook (10 Feb 2025) sets 10 principles — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-playbook-for-the-uk-government)
- **Australia (DTA)**, 13 October 2025: staff guidance — "Don't put security classified information – OFFICIAL: Sensitive or above – into these tools", "Never enter personal information", follow agency ICT security policies; agency guidance "Managing access to public generative AI tools" — "It remains up to agencies to decide whether to enable access", with recommendations to provide "training and safeguards that build workforce capability", "Monitoring use, requiring human oversight and recording AI-supported decisions", and "Prioritising enterprise-grade AI solutions for sensitive or classified material" — [DTA](https://www.dta.gov.au/media-releases/dta-releases-new-guidance-australian-government-use-public-generative-ai-tools)
- **US (GSA, OMB M-25-21/M-25-22 compliance plan, 30 Sep 2025)**: an AI Oversight Committee "reviews every AI request" and sorts use cases into familiarization, pre-acquisition, R&D and production; the EDGE Board approves high-impact use cases; production systems need an Authorization to Operate, privacy assessments and public listing in the use-case inventory; datasets must be registered in the Enterprise Data Solution catalog ("Sensitive data cannot be used without explicit clearance and a valid Authorization to Operate"); the internal inventory captures funding sources, technical scope, data requirements and risk profiles; annual CAIO request plus updates "as capabilities change or systems are decommissioned"; discovery via ATO, FITARA review and new-software request processes. No coding-assistant or citizen-development policy — [GSA](https://www.gsa.gov/technology/government-it-initiatives/artificial-intelligence/ai-guidance-and-resources/ai-compliance-plan)
- **Sweden**: Digg and IMY national guidelines for generative AI in public administration, launched 21 January 2025 under a July 2024 government assignment; cover Management and Accountability, Cyber Security, GDPR, Ethics, Labour Law and Procurement; "personal data questions… need to be identified and taken care of early in the process when AI is introduced". Nothing on code generation or app building in the English summary — [IMY](https://www.imy.se/en/news/national-guidelines-for-generative-ai-in-public-administration-are-launched/)

### Inferences
- Bounded statement the report can make: "Of about 40 sources reviewed in October 2026, three organisations (Method, Tenable, EXANTE) describe their own vibe-coding governance in the first person; none of them is a bank, telco, public body or EU-headquartered large enterprise, and none publishes an approval SLA, registry schema, expiry rule or outcome metric. One unnamed European insurer is described second-hand by AWS, for professional developers." That replaces the original "no company publishes a practice".
- All three disclosures converge on the same primitives — governed APIs/connectors instead of direct data access, a managed runtime, version control, review before production, training tied to access — which supports the *direction* of the paved-road thesis but is three self-reports, not comparative outcome evidence.
- Government guidance governs *use of AI tools by staff*; the citizen-built *app* as an artefact with an owner and lifecycle is absent from every government document reviewed. The GSA model (use case → dataset registry → ATO → public inventory) is the closest public-sector analogue to an app registry.

### Gaps
- No bank, telco, utility, hospital group or EU large enterprise publishing a vibe-coding approval workflow or acceptable-use policy was found. The audience for such material (CISO conferences, internal wikis) rarely publishes.
- The Danish Digitaliseringsstyrelsen guide on generative AI (digst.dk) was located but not read; Digg's full Swedish guidelines were not read.
- No source found for a named organisation that specifically blocks Lovable/Replit/Bolt/Base44 for employees in 2025–26.

## Key question 3: What should an app registry schema contain, and how does it link to data-protection records without overclaiming?

### Takeaway
Three documented registries exist: the Power Platform CoE Starter Kit's compliance form (business justification, business impact, access management including joiners/movers/leavers, dependencies, conditions of use, mitigation plan), Cloudflare's metadata store (owner, team, description, connected data sources, usage metrics, cost via tags) and ServiceNow's citizen-development intake (repeatability, expected users, sensitive data, cross-department data, data access). None links a registry entry to a GDPR Article 30 record; the only public model that chains an app/use-case record to a dataset registry and privacy assessment is GSA's federal compliance plan.

### Cited Findings
- **Power Platform CoE Starter Kit "Support Details" form** (fields a maker must submit to clear "Missing Details"): "Business Justification: Describe the business need and the problem you're solving"; "Business Impact: Define the operational impact this solution has on the people using it"; "Access Management: Describe who has access to this resource, how access is managed (individual user access or access through group membership), and how joiners/movers/leavers processes are managed"; "Dependencies: Describe any dependencies this solution uses—for example, external or internal APIs or Azure resources"; "Conditions of use" (connectors only); "Mitigation Plan provided: For critical solutions, upload a mitigation plan that details what business users do if there isn't an outage". Microsoft tells adopters to "Verify that the Support details form matches your requirements, and update it if necessary." — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/guidance/coe/governance-components)
- The Starter Kit's inventory tables sync apps, flows, makers, connectors and environments, and the compliance triggers use fields for share count ("shared with more than 20 users or at least one group"), last-published date (60 days), description presence, business-impact rating and attachments — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/guidance/coe/governance-components)
- **Cloudflare reference architecture** registry fields: "the owner, team, description, connected data sources, and usage metrics for each deployment"; cost attribution via resource tags per user worker; development-plane metadata store holds user profiles, sessions, cost tags, and department/team permissions — [Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/reference-architecture/diagrams/ai/enterprise-ai-vibe-coding-platform/)
- **ServiceNow citizen-development intake form** questions: repeatability, current manual processes, expected user count, sensitive data, cross-department data needs, data access; the programme manager checks for duplicate functionality, platform fit, licensing, suitability for citizen development and developer skills; before build, the team assigns who supports and who maintains the app in production (citizen developer, their manager, or the core ServiceNow team) and who owns documentation — [ServiceNow Community](https://www.servicenow.com/community/coe-developer-citizen-articles/citizen-development-define-the-application-lifecycle/ta-p/2494868)
- **Mendix**: every deployed app has exactly one Technical Contact ("It is not possible to have more than one Technical Contact for an app"), transferable for licensed apps; Private Mendix Platform admins can "Assign the app to a new owner", assign ownership to a group, archive or delete — [Mendix App Roles](https://docs.mendix.com/developerportal/general/app-roles/); [Private Mendix Platform admin](https://docs.mendix.com/private-mendix-platform/reference-guide/admin/company/)
- **CSA** asks for a "lightweight registration process" and, for the agentic tier, flags AI API calls/agentic functionality as a classification criterion; the CSA shadow-AI note asks for an "AI asset owner" and data-tier mapping (public/internal/confidential/restricted) with restricted data requiring "explicit written approval" — [CSA vibe coding](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-vibe-coding-ai-governance-gap-20260602-csa/); [CSA shadow AI](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/CSA_research_note_shadow_ai_apps_enterprise_20260530-csa-styled.pdf)
- **GSA** (public sector): the internal AI inventory captures "funding sources, technical scope, data requirements, and risk profiles"; datasets must be registered in the Enterprise Data Solution catalog; production use requires ATO plus privacy assessments; entries are updated when systems are decommissioned — [GSA](https://www.gsa.gov/technology/government-it-initiatives/artificial-intelligence/ai-guidance-and-resources/ai-compliance-plan)
- Power Platform admin centre's own "valid owner" check only covers apps "active in the last 90 days" in managed environments, and "New owners don't automatically get permissions to the environment or data sources used in the app" — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/admin/security-recommendations)

### Inferences
- A defensible schema, assembled only from fields that appear in at least one documented registry: owner (person) + backup/support owner; team/department; description/business justification; business impact rating; audience and share scope (count/groups/"Everyone"); data sources and connectors; data classification tier; sensitive-data flag; cross-department data flag; AI-feature/agentic flag (CSA tier 3 criterion); dependencies (APIs, cloud resources); access-management description including JML handling; mitigation/continuity plan for critical apps; cost attribution tag; last-published and last-used dates; usage metrics; environment/platform. Fields in the original report that no documented registry has: expiry date and cost centre as a dedicated field (Cloudflare uses tags).
- On data-protection linkage: the correct, non-overclaiming formulation is that the registry stores a *reference* to the processing-activity record and the DPIA-screening outcome, because Article 30 records are kept per processing activity (per the audit) and several apps may serve one activity. The GSA pattern (app/use case → registered dataset → privacy assessment → authorisation) is the only published chain of this kind and is public-sector-specific. No private-sector source documents a registry-to-ROPA link; this remains author synthesis.

### Gaps
- No published registry includes an expiry/TTL field; no published registry includes a DPIA/ROPA reference field. Both must be labelled as proposals.
- The Starter Kit is "no longer actively maintained"; whether the Power Platform admin centre's native inventory exposes equivalent justification fields was not verified.

## Key question 4: What lifecycle controls (expiry, recertification, offboarding, quarantine, versioning, exceptions, supplier review) exist in any platform or published policy, with specifics?

### Takeaway
The only fully specified lifecycle machinery in the public record is Microsoft's Power Platform CoE Starter Kit (now unmaintained, with parts moved into the admin centre): six-month inactivity → owner approval → deletion three weeks later, manager escalation after a month of silence, orphaned-object reassignment to the former owner's manager, and quarantine after 7 days of non-compliance with admin risk assessment before release. Lovable auto-transfers a leaver's projects to the most senior remaining member (except Restricted projects); Mendix offers archive/un-archive and single-owner transfer; ServiceNow's guidance sets "at least once a year" reviews and a back-up-then-delete retirement; Method deletes previews when PRs close. No source specifies an app TTL, a recertification cadence tied to data class, or an exception register.

### Cited Findings

**Inactivity and expiry**
- CoE Starter Kit inactivity flows: weekly check "for apps not modified or launched in the last six months (configurable)"; the owner is asked via flow approval "whether the app can be deleted" and advised to back it up; a daily flow "Deletes the flows and apps approved for deletion more than three weeks ago (configurable)"; ignored requests do not delete the app but recur, and after "one month or more" the maker's manager receives a list via the "Cleanup Old Objects App", from which the manager can "reject the deletion or send a reminder"; admins can "exempt the object from future runs" — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/guidance/coe/governance-components)
- Dataverse for Teams environments: deleted if created "More than 7 days ago and have no business justification, or the business justification is rejected", or "More than 90 days ago and have no apps or flows"; owners have seven days to justify; deleted environments recoverable within seven days. Microsoft notes automatic deletion of inactive Teams environments is now native in the admin centre — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/guidance/coe/governance-components)
- Method: preview deployments on temporary subdomains are "cleaned up automatically when the PR closes" — the only automatic expiry in a vibe-coding-specific disclosure — [Method](https://methodfi.com/resources/method-labs/vibe)
- Mendix: an app "no longer needed for a time" can be archived ("no longer available to users, but its data is still stored, and it can be quickly un-archived"); deletion "removes its repository, so that it cannot be restored"; licence expiry gives a 30-day grace period after which "the app may be forcefully stopped by the Mendix runtime" — [Private Mendix Platform admin](https://docs.mendix.com/private-mendix-platform/reference-guide/admin/company/); [Mendix Deployed Apps](https://docs.mendix.com/control-center/deployed-apps/)

**Quarantine / kill switch**
- CoE Starter Kit app quarantine: environment variable "Quarantine Apps after x days of noncompliance", default 7 days; daily flow quarantines an app when compliance details "have been requested, but aren't yet submitted, and are pending longer than specified", the environment is in scope, the app is not already quarantined and the "Admin Risk Assessment status isn't complete". A quarantined app "will present end users who launch the app a message indicating they're unable to access the app" (canvas apps only). If the maker submits details "the app is automatically released from quarantine"; otherwise "An admin must perform a risk assessment and manually release the app" — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/guidance/coe/governance-components); [quarantine setup](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/guidance/coe/setup-quarantine-components)
- Qovery (vendor claim, unverified in use): "An emergency kill switch immediately halts all agent traffic org-wide or per-blueprint", with off/monitor/enforce modes — [Qovery](https://www.qovery.com/blog/the-lovable-experience-enterprise-governance-your-infrastructure-we-built-it)

**Owner departure, transfer and offboarding**
- CoE Starter Kit orphaned objects: a weekly parent flow "collects all orphaned objects in the tenant and attempts to associate them with the manager of the former owner", sends a Teams card, and a child flow lets the manager "Email themselves the list", "Take ownership of them all", "Delete them all", "Assign them all to someone else" or review individually; unresolved objects go to the admin mailbox. Not available in GCC High/DoD (no adaptive cards) — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/guidance/coe/governance-components); [setup](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/guidance/coe/setup-orphan-components)
- Power Platform admin centre (native): "Assign valid owners to apps to mitigate business continuity risks" lists ownerless apps in managed environments active in the last 90 days; actions "Assign to new owner" and "Promote co-owner to owner"; severity High, refreshed weekly, managed environments only; "New owners don't automatically get permissions to the environment or data sources used in the app. Admins must manually give owners permission." — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/admin/security-recommendations)
- Lovable workspaces: when a member leaves, "Membership ends immediately" and access to all workspace projects is lost, but "Your work is not deleted"; owned projects "pass automatically to the most senior remaining member" in the order owners, admins, editors; "Projects with access set to Restricted are not transferred automatically"; "You cannot pick which member inherits your projects"; on Enterprise "the leave event is recorded in audit logs"; project transfer by editors on Enterprise requires the "Editor project transfers" setting; workspace deletion has a 60-day grace period. No inventory or expiry controls documented — [Lovable docs](https://docs.lovable.dev/features/workspace.md)
- Mendix: recommends SSO with the corporate IdP so "Former employees will not be able to login"; Technical Contact role is transferable for licensed apps; Private Mendix Platform audit log records "App owner edited", "App archived", "App member removed" — [Mendix governance](https://docs.mendix.com/developerportal/digital-execution/governance-control/); [Private Mendix Platform admin](https://docs.mendix.com/private-mendix-platform/reference-guide/admin/company/)
- ServiceNow: ownership (support vs maintenance) assigned before build and "reassessed whenever the app is significantly enhanced"; apps growing in complexity "may need a professional developer"; no formal owner-transfer procedure described — [ServiceNow Community](https://www.servicenow.com/community/coe-developer-citizen-articles/citizen-development-define-the-application-lifecycle/ta-p/2494868)

**Recertification / periodic review**
- ServiceNow: "Run an application and user review regularly, at least once a year" — [ServiceNow Community](https://www.servicenow.com/community/coe-developer-citizen-articles/citizen-development-define-the-application-lifecycle/ta-p/2494868)
- CoE Starter Kit "Re-publish needed" status flags canvas apps "not published in the past 60 days" — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/guidance/coe/governance-components)
- Power Platform admin centre: "Periodically review overshared apps and adjust permissions" (apps shared with Everyone, weekly refresh) — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/admin/security-recommendations)
- GSA: annual CAIO inventory request plus event-driven updates — [GSA](https://www.gsa.gov/technology/government-it-initiatives/artificial-intelligence/ai-guidance-and-resources/ai-compliance-plan)

**Versioning and change control**
- EXANTE: "We insist that all vibe-coded work goes into version control" — [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/thought-leadership/vibe-coding-in-a-regulated-business-or-how-i-learned-to-stop-worrying-and-let-the-compliance-team-have-kittens/)
- ServiceNow: "Significant modifications go through the same lifecycle phases as new apps. Minor fixes may take a faster path"; deployment via Update Sets or Application Repository remains the core team's responsibility "including security configuration" — [ServiceNow Community](https://www.servicenow.com/community/coe-developer-citizen-articles/citizen-development-define-the-application-lifecycle/ta-p/2494868)
- Method: every app change is a PR with automated scanning, AI review and human approval — [Method](https://methodfi.com/resources/method-labs/vibe); Cloudflare: generated code can flow through existing enterprise git repositories and code-review flows — [Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/reference-architecture/diagrams/ai/enterprise-ai-vibe-coding-platform/)

**Decommissioning**
- ServiceNow: evaluate unused apps for retirement; "Notify previous users where possible"; "Assess technical dependencies on the app or its data, and resolve them"; "Back up the app and its data, store them according to enterprise policy, and then delete the app" — [ServiceNow Community](https://www.servicenow.com/community/coe-developer-citizen-articles/citizen-development-define-the-application-lifecycle/ta-p/2494868)

**Exception management**
- CoE Starter Kit: admins can mark an object "excused" with an approval comment to exempt it from future inactivity runs, and environments can be exempted from compliance and inactivity processes — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/guidance/coe/governance-components); [tenant hygiene](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/guidance/coe/after-setup-tenant-hygiene)
- GSA: waivers "granted by the CAIO with written justification, reported to OMB within 30 days, and reviewed annually"; "GSA has not issued a waiver at the time of this compliance plan's publication" — [GSA](https://www.gsa.gov/technology/government-it-initiatives/artificial-intelligence/ai-guidance-and-resources/ai-compliance-plan)

**Third-party / supplier review**
- CSA: where source export is unsupported, "require vendor security attestations and audit logging in contracts" — [CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-vibe-coding-ai-governance-gap-20260602-csa/)
- Gartner: "incorporate Gen AI risk evaluation into their SaaS assessment processes" — [Gartner newsroom](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-11-19-gartner-identifies-critical-genai-blind-spots-that-cios-must-urgently-address0)
- Tandem (bank compliance vendor, secondary): "make sure third-party AI vendors are included in your vendor management program… review each AI vendor's privacy policy, SOC report" — [Tandem](https://tandem.app/blog/vibe-coding-and-ai-citizen-risk-management-tips-for-banks-and)

### Inferences
- The lifecycle controls the original report proposed (quarantine on owner departure or missed attestation; expiry enforced on prototypes; quarterly attestation) are all *analogues* of Starter Kit mechanisms, with two differences that must be stated: the Starter Kit's triggers are inactivity and missing compliance details, not data class or calendar expiry; and its defaults (6 months, 7 days, 3 weeks, 60 days, 20 users) are configurable examples, not industry norms.
- Lovable's auto-inheritance on leave is a double-edged lifecycle control: it prevents orphaning but assigns ownership by seniority rather than by business relevance, and Restricted projects can still be orphaned.
- No source defines approval SLAs for citizen apps. The GSA plan has process stages but no timing.

### Gaps
- No platform or policy publishes a TTL for prototype apps, a recertification cadence linked to data class, or a documented exception register for vibe-coded apps.
- Replit and Base44 offboarding/ownership-transfer behaviour was not verified in this pass.
- Whether the Power Platform admin centre's native features reproduce the Starter Kit's quarantine-after-N-days automation was not verified.

## Key question 6: What are the strongest arguments against the paved-road/amnesty model, and what evidence supports them?

### Takeaway
The strongest documented counter-arguments are: (1) the sanctioned platform itself becomes the shadow estate (Power Platform tenants with ~80,000 apps, 63% of copilots overshared, up to 30% of vulnerable apps orphaned, "credentials sharing as a service" via personal connections, governance dashboards "frequently underutilized"); (2) maintenance and cost burden shifts to engineering (Gartner's 60%/30% forecasts, Tenable's "overwhelming" volume and token/data-lake sprawl); (3) blocking is mainstream and coexists with enablement (90% of Netskope organisations block some genAI apps), so "bans fail" is not what the data shows — personal-account use fell from 78% to 47% while blocking stayed high; (4) non-punitive registration without enforcement has no documented effect, and the CSA itself notes policy without communication "may have negligible effect". No comparative outcome study of ban vs amnesty vs paved road exists.

### Cited Findings

**The paved road becomes its own shadow surface**
- CSA: sanctioned-platform governance dashboards are "frequently underutilized"; approved access and governed use are "two distinct states" — [CSA vibe coding](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-vibe-coding-ai-governance-gap-20260602-csa/); [CSA shadow AI](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/CSA_research_note_shadow_ai_apps_enterprise_20260530-csa-styled.pdf)
- Zenity (customer telemetry, 2024): ~80,000 apps/copilots per large enterprise versus ~500 SaaS apps; 62% with vulnerabilities; 63% of 2,600+ copilots overshared; 6,200 privileged guests — [PR Newswire / Zenity](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/zenity-research-finds-62-of-copilots-and-low-code-apps-contain-security-vulnerabilities-302241231.html)
- Nokod (customer sample, 2026): medium environments "approximately 700 vulnerable applications that are owned by 200 different individuals", large "around 10,000 vulnerable applications owned by 2,500 individuals"; up to 30% orphaned — [Nokod](https://nokodsecurity.com/blog/the-uncomfortable-truth-about-application-security-in-the-age-of-no-coders-lessons-from-cve-2026-20960/)
- Practitioner critique (Jukka Niiranen): Power Platform connections create "technical dependencies to individual users… (a.k.a. 'credentials sharing as a service')"; there are no migration tools to move apps "somewhere safer"; the harder problem is "what's used by whom, where does it belong, who's gonna take care of it… information that no one has gathered before"; Microsoft's own tenant (30,000 environments) is managed by five people — [Perspectives+](https://www.perspectives.plus/p/one-billion-apps)

**Maintenance, cost and capacity burden**
- Gartner forecast: 60% of vibe-coded apps retired by 2028; 30% of engineering teams under "material capacity strain" — [Tray.ai](https://tray.ai/guides/gartner-govern-vibe-coding.md)
- Tenable (first-party): "Even when 'citizen coders' comply… the number of applications they build using AI tools can become overwhelming and disruptive for the IT and security teams"; "hundreds of employees building applications… IT and security suddenly face the daunting task of assessing each one"; token costs; "siloed data lakes creates dangerous data sprawl" — [Tenable](https://www.tenable.com/blog/how-to-secure-mitigate-ai-risk-vibe-coding-ai-apps-citizen-coders)

**Blocking is mainstream and coexists with enablement; "bans fail" is under-evidenced**
- Netskope 2026 (telemetry, 1 Oct 2024–31 Oct 2025): "90% of organizations block at least some genAI apps. The average organization actively blocks 10" (ZeroGPT 45%, DeepSeek 43%); over the same period personal-account genAI use fell from 78% to 47% and organisation-managed use rose from 25% to 62%, while apps used per org rose from 6 to 8 — [Netskope](https://www.netskope.com/resources/cloud-and-threat-reports/cloud-and-threat-report-2026). (A fetched summary of the report also attributed to it the statement that block policies "have generally been very effective" and that more organisations will move to allowlists; a second pass could only confirm the recommendation "to allow reputable apps currently in use while blocking all others". Verify exact wording before quoting.)
- 2023 corporate bans were temporary/partial restrictions, not evaluated outcomes: Samsung "temporarily restrict[ed]" genAI on company devices after engineers uploaded source code, and asked staff using personal devices "to not submit any company-related information"; JPMorgan, Verizon, Bank of America, Citi, Deutsche Bank, Goldman Sachs and Wells Fargo restricted ChatGPT — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/05/02/samsung-bans-use-of-generative-ai-tools-like-chatgpt-after-april-internal-data-leak); [Straits Times/Bloomberg](https://www.straitstimes.com/asia/east-asia/samsung-bans-staff-s-ai-use-after-spotting-chatgpt-data-leak); [Fox Business/WSJ](https://www.foxbusiness.com/technology/jpmorgan-restricts-employees-using-chatgpt). No source was found measuring what happened to leakage at these firms afterwards.
- Stated intent to defy bans (self-report, not behaviour): 46% of 6,000 knowledge workers (US/UK/Germany) "would keep using personal AI tools even if their organizations banned them completely" (Software AG, Nov 2024) — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/unauthorized-ai-use-surges-at-work/); 66% of 1,250 office professionals had used AI believing policy did not allow it (PagerDuty/Wakefield, Apr 2026) — [Pebblous summary](https://blog.pebblous.ai/blog/shadow-ai-konstanz-workplace-survey/en/)
- Personal-account use persists even in sanctioned-tool categories: 32.3% of ChatGPT, 24.9% of Gemini, 58.2% of Claude and 60.9% of Perplexity usage via personal accounts; 39.7% of AI interactions involve sensitive data (Cyberhaven, 222 companies, 2025) — [Cyberhaven PDF](https://info.cyberhaven.com/hubfs/Webflow_Resources/Cyberhaven-AI-Risk-Report-2026.pdf)

**Non-punitive registration and training lack outcome evidence**
- CSA: a policy "may have negligible effect" without active communication; only awareness is claimed as the goal of registration — [CSA shadow AI](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/CSA_research_note_shadow_ai_apps_enterprise_20260530-csa-styled.pdf); [CSA vibe coding](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-vibe-coding-ai-governance-gap-20260602-csa/)
- Secondary trade-press position cited by the original report ("consequences for noncompliance") sits against CSA's "non-punitive"; neither side offers data.

### Inferences
- The honest framing is: paved road + registration is the consensus *recommendation* of Gartner, CSA, Tenable, Method, EXANTE and Cloudflare, and the consensus *practice* measured by Netskope is "block many, sanction some" — the two are compatible, and the original report's binary "govern, don't block" misdescribed what organisations do.
- The best-evidenced structural critique is that a sanctioned platform without lifecycle automation reproduces the shadow problem at larger scale; this strengthens the case for the lifecycle controls in Q4 rather than against the paved road.

### Gaps
- No comparative study of ban vs amnesty vs paved-road outcomes exists; all evidence is self-report, vendor telemetry or forecast.
- The Cyberhaven-sourced piece "Shadow AI is a governance gap, not a training failure" (NHI Mgmt Group, June 2026) could not be fetched (robots.txt).

## Supplementary B: Training and AI-literacy programmes for citizen builders

### Takeaway
Every source that addresses training recommends it; none presents outcome evidence that training reduces incidents among citizen builders. The specific, documented practices are: tying tool access to mandatory training (Tenable), platform-specific scenario-based content (CSA), mandatory responsible-AI training for key roles at maturity Level 300+ and "% of employees trained" as a key result (Microsoft), and workforce-capability training as a government recommendation (DTA). The KPMG data shows training and policy are *absent* for about half of employees in advanced economies, which is correlational context, not proof of efficacy.

### Cited Findings
- Tenable: "Tenable has tied AI tool access directly to mandatory security training and responsible usage training, and in turn complements it with live webinars, pre-built courses, tool-specific deep dives, and weekly show-and-tell sessions"; attributes most citizen-coder issues to "a lack of knowledge about how to prevent these problems" — [Tenable](https://www.tenable.com/blog/how-to-secure-mitigate-ai-risk-vibe-coding-ai-apps-citizen-coders)
- CSA: "Training should be concrete, scenario-based, and tied to the specific platforms employees are using." — [CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-vibe-coding-ai-governance-gap-20260602-csa/)
- Microsoft adoption maturity model: Level 300 opportunity "Make responsible AI training mandatory for key roles, and establish a dedicated community of practice"; Level 400 "Responsible AI training is mandatory for key roles"; nurture components cover onboarding new makers, training workshops, hackathons, templates; example key result "___% of employees trained and skilled in low-code development" — [Microsoft Learn maturity goals](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/guidance/adoption/maturity-model-goals); [CoE overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/guidance/coe/overview); [vision](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/guidance/adoption/vision)
- DTA: "Providing training and safeguards that build workforce capability" — [DTA](https://www.dta.gov.au/media-releases/dta-releases-new-guidance-australian-government-use-public-generative-ai-tools)
- UK AICA guidance: "The programmer is responsible for code produced"; "the responsible use of AI coding assistants require you to understand the resulting code" — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-insights/ai-insights-ai-coding-assistants-for-developers-in-hmg-html)
- Secure Code Warrior CEO (vendor opinion, SD Times, 10 Sep 2026): citizen developers "need a level of AI literacy that they don't have"; training should cover where API keys are stored, who can access them, and documenting which systems an integration connects to; phased model (AI-assisted → AI-native code reviewer → agentic policy enforcement). No outcome data — [SD Times](https://sdtimes.com/developer-training/citizen-developers-are-the-new-enterprise-threat-vector-so-why-is-nobody-warning-them/)
- KPMG/Melbourne: "only one in two employees in advanced economies report that their organization offers training in responsible AI, has policies and practices on responsible AI use"; "only two in five say there is a policy guiding its use"; 66% rely on AI output without evaluating it; 56% made work mistakes due to AI — [KPMG global report PDF](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/xx/pdf/2025/05/trust-attitudes-and-use-of-ai-global-report.pdf)
- Software AG: only 27% of shadow-AI users "routinely run security scans on their tools" and 29% review data usage policies — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/unauthorized-ai-use-surges-at-work/)
- Both Tenable and the CSA point to the OWASP Citizen Development Top 10 as the content baseline, with the CSA noting it "predate[s] vibe coding" — [Tenable](https://www.tenable.com/blog/how-to-secure-mitigate-ai-risk-vibe-coding-ai-apps-citizen-coders); [CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-vibe-coding-ai-governance-gap-20260602-csa/)

### Inferences
- The only training design with a documented enforcement hook is "no training, no tool access" (Tenable); it is the one pattern that converts training from advisory to a gate.
- Training content that matches the incident record (BaaS keys and authorisation, publishing defaults, secrets placement, connector scope) is recommended by the CSA's "platform-specific" principle but no public curriculum for vibe-coding citizen builders was found.

### Gaps
- No controlled or before/after study of citizen-developer or vibe-coder training effectiveness was located.
- The EU AI Act Article 4 literacy obligation's current consolidated wording post-Omnibus was not re-checked in this pass (the audit flagged this separately).

## Supplementary C: Status of the Power Platform CoE Starter Kit as a template

### Takeaway
The CoE Starter Kit remains the most complete public specification of citizen-development lifecycle governance, but Microsoft now states it "is no longer actively maintained" and that its core capabilities have moved into the Power Platform admin centre; some Starter Kit functions (bulk permission updates, cleaning up abandoned resources, surveying makers, ROI calculation) are described as not yet matched by managed environments. The report should cite the Starter Kit as a reference design, not as a currently supported product.

### Cited Findings
- "The Power Platform CoE Starter Kit is no longer actively maintained. Its core capabilities are part of the Power Platform admin center. Issues are no longer reviewed or addressed." — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/guidance/coe/governance-components)
- "The CoE Starter Kit offers capabilities that aren't yet met with managed environments, such as bulk updating permissions, cleaning up abandoned resources, surveying makers, and calculating the ROI of ideas." "The CoE Starter Kit doesn't limit sharing on resources. Proactive governance is only possible through managed environments." — [Microsoft Learn CoE overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/guidance/coe/overview)
- Planning questions Microsoft poses for a governance strategy: "What are the requirements of a compliant app or maker? What information do you need per app or maker? What happens to noncompliant apps and makers? How do you plan to support your maker community…? What actions do you plan to take based on your collected information?" — [Microsoft Learn CoE overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/guidance/coe/overview)
- Native admin-centre successors: inventory of apps, flows and agents; Usage page; Actions/Advisor page with weekly security recommendations; automatic deletion of inactive Dataverse for Teams environments — [Microsoft Learn tenant hygiene](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/guidance/coe/after-setup-tenant-hygiene); [security recommendations](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/admin/security-recommendations)

### Inferences
- The specific numbers in the Starter Kit (6 months, 7 days, 3 weeks, 60 days, 20 users, 50 launches) are Microsoft's shipped defaults and the only published, mechanised thresholds for citizen-app lifecycle anywhere; they are a reasonable starting point for a vibe-coding registry policy if labelled as borrowed defaults.

### Gaps
- Whether the admin centre's native "Actions" now automate quarantine and inactivity deletion with equivalent thresholds was not verified.
